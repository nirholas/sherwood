The price is the part everyone gets wrong

A tokenized share is not the share. It trades 24/7 on a chain while the underlying trades for six and a half hours on a weekday. Any lending protocol has to pick which of those two prices it believes, and both obvious answers are wrong.

Settle on what a liquidator can realise

A liquidator seizes tokens and sells them into a pool on this chain. What they get there, not what the share prints for on the NYSE, is what makes a liquidation solvent. So the settlement price is the pool TWAP.

Bound it with the exchange

A quorum of reporters attests the real equity price. It is never used as the price, only as a bound on the pool. Move the pool and it walks away from the attestation; compromise the reporters and they walk away from the pool. Either way the feed goes dark.

Neither source can move the price alone

That is the whole security argument, and it inverts the usual oracle: the trustless source decides, and the trusted one can only ever refuse to confirm. A compromised signer cannot mint value out of nothing.

→
The band is measured, not guessed. Across all 38 listed equities the on-chain token tracks its underlying to a median of 65 basis points and a 90th percentile of 163. The intraday band is 200bps and the off-hours band is 500, because a token that trades all night legitimately reprices ahead of an open that has not happened yet.

What happens when trading stops

Every Robinhood stock token can be paused by its issuer. While it is, transfer reverts, so collateral cannot be seized and a liquidation is not merely discouraged, it is impossible. A lending protocol built for assets that never stop gets this catastrophically wrong.

Without a halt shield

Interest compounds through a two-day halt against borrowers who could not repay or top up. Nobody can liquidate, so nothing clears. At the unpause the entire book is underwater at once, at a price that gapped while nobody could act, and the first liquidator in the block takes all of it at a discount.

What Sherwood does

  • Interest stops accruing entirely. A halt costs the borrower nothing.
  • New borrows and collateral withdrawals are refused; health cannot be evaluated.
  • Repaying and supplying stay open, because they only reduce risk.
  • Depositors can still withdraw. A halt is not a reason to trap people who are owed money.
  • When the price returns, the liquidation bonus ramps from zero over four hours.
⚠
The grace ramp is the point. At the first second after a halt clears there is no profit in liquidating anyone, so nobody does, and a borrower who was locked out gets a real chance to cure at the new price. By the end of the window the full incentive is back and the market clears normally. The protocol trades some liquidation speed for not confiscating positions over an event the borrower could not respond to.

Corporate actions, read off the token

Robinhood's stock tokens publish a uiMultiplier, and they publish the next one along with the second it takes effect. That gives Sherwood something no mainnet price feed has: advance, on-chain notice of a split. It is used twice.

Before it lands

A scheduled fall in the multiplier is a scheduled fall in what a raw token is worth. It is applied to collateral the moment it is announced, so nobody borrows against value that is already booked to disappear. A scheduled rise is not credited early.

Just after it lands

For one TWAP window the moving average straddles the jump and is wrong by up to the split ratio. The feed reports MultiplierTransition and serves nothing. A protocol that missed this would liquidate its whole book at a tenth of fair value the morning after a reverse split.

Baskets diversify halt risk, not just returns

Spread the same collateral across ten tickers and one halt no longer freezes the position, it removes one name's weight. That improvement is a mechanical property of the collateral rather than an assumption about correlation, which is why basket markets can run at a higher loan-to-value than any of their constituents would justify alone.

A halted constituent is carried at zero, never at its last price, so a basket's reported value is always a floor on the truth. And a redemption pays out every name it can move and books the rest as a claim, so one frozen ticker delays a payout instead of trapping a liquidator.
